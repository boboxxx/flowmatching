\title{Virtual Retransmission via Flow Matching for Reliable DeepJSCC Image Transmission}
\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}\IEEEauthorblockA{Anonymous submission}}

\begin{abstract}
We introduce FlowHARQ, a receiver-side virtual retransmission that uses
flow matching before deciding whether to request another wireless round.
FlowHARQ detects unreliable DeepJSCC latent tokens, transports only those
tokens toward the clean latent distribution, and preserves every trusted token
exactly.  Robust velocity supervision makes this repair effective under
deep-fade outliers, while a target-aware quality head converts the post-flow
state into ACK/NACK.  Across three training and three channel seeds on 80
held-out DIV2K images, FlowHARQ reduces physical retransmissions by
\MainRetxDelta{} percentage points relative to adaptive HARQ (paired 95\%
CI [\MainRetxCILow{}, \MainRetxCIHigh{}]) while changing PSNR by
\MainPSNRDelta{} dB and LPIPS by \MainLPIPSDelta{}.  Without any refitting,
Kodak24 replicates a \ExternalRetxDelta{}-point change (95\% CI
[\ExternalRetxCILow{}, \ExternalRetxCIHigh{}]).  The results demonstrate
that receiver computation can replace a small but statistically supported
fraction of physical HARQ rounds.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Deep joint source--channel coding (DeepJSCC) maps images directly to channel
symbols and degrades gracefully with SNR \cite{deepjscc}.  Transformer
backbones further improve high-resolution transmission and channel
adaptability \cite{swinjscc}.  Reliability remains difficult in severe fading:
always retransmitting wastes airtime, whereas an erroneous ACK produces a poor
reconstruction.  Recent semantic HARQ methods learn when or what to retransmit
\cite{djscc_h,fineharq}, but recovery still consumes another wireless round.

Flow matching (FM) learns a continuous vector field without simulating an ODE
during training \cite{flowmatching}.  Existing FM communication receivers use
FM for global reconstruction or refinement \cite{flowsem,rcbfm}.  Their goal is
better or faster decoding, including channel-aware latent transport
\cite{channel_lfm}, not deciding whether a physical retransmission can be
skipped.  Conversely, generative semantic HARQ has been explored for text
through stochastic VAE latents \cite{genharq}, leaving image DeepJSCC and FM
unconnected at the HARQ decision point.

We introduce \emph{virtual retransmission}: the receiver repairs unreliable
semantic latents before issuing ACK/NACK.  Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
 \item an FM-before-HARQ architecture in which receiver computation may avoid
 a physical channel round;
 \item reliability-anchored partial transport with a hard identity constraint
 on trusted tokens and robust, reconstruction-aligned velocity training; and
 \item leakage-free paired evaluation against adaptive HARQ under
 time-correlated fading, including cross-seed confidence intervals, latency,
 and external Kodak24 validation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\subsection{DeepJSCC and Semantic HARQ}
DeepJSCC replaces separately optimized compression and channel coding with a
learned analog mapping \cite{deepjscc}; SwinJSCC extends this idea using a
hierarchical Transformer and rate/SNR adaptation \cite{swinjscc}.  Reliability
extensions have largely focused on the physical retransmission itself.
DJSCC-H adapts the HARQ process for image delivery \cite{djscc_h}, while
fine-grained semantic HARQ identifies and retransmits erroneous semantic bases
\cite{fineharq}.  Generative semantic HARQ combines stochastic VAE latents,
but its demonstrated modality is text \cite{genharq}.  FlowHARQ instead asks
whether receiver-side generative repair can make a physical image
retransmission unnecessary; the conference version retains full second-round
retransmission when repair is insufficient.

\subsection{Flow Matching for Semantic Communication}
Flow matching learns conditional transport by regressing a prescribed path
velocity \cite{flowmatching}.  FlowSem conditions global image generation on a
coarse DeepJSCC reconstruction \cite{flowsem}.  RC-BFM begins from a
channel-induced semantic state to shorten generative decoding
\cite{rcbfm}, and channel-aware latent FM incorporates communication state
directly into latent transport \cite{channel_lfm}.  These works use FM as a
decoder or refinement mechanism.  FlowHARQ uses a hard-masked latent flow as
an intermediate reliability action and evaluates it by the physical rounds
that it avoids.  Mobility-aware DeepJSCC has separately studied time-varying
MIMO adaptation \cite{mobility_deepjscc}; here mobility controls the
correlation between paired HARQ rounds.

\section{System Model}
For image $\mathbf x$, a SwinJSCC encoder emits $N$ real-valued latent tokens
$\mathbf Z=E_\theta(\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R^{N\times d}$.  Adjacent latent
dimensions form complex symbols.  In HARQ round $r\in\{1,2\}$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf y_r=h_r\mathbf s+\mathbf n_r,\qquad
 \widetilde{\mathbf Z}_r=\mathcal Q^{-1}(h_r^*\mathbf y_r/|h_r|^2),
 \label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal Q^{-1}$ restores token scale and the receiver knows $h_r$.
Fading between rounds follows
\begin{equation}
 h_2=\rho_\Delta h_1+\sqrt{1-\rho_\Delta^2}\,e,\quad
 \rho_\Delta=J_0(2\pi f_D\Delta),
 \label{eq:jakes}
\end{equation}
with $e\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)$ and HARQ interval $\Delta$.  Thus speed changes
the joint, not merely marginal, round statistics.  A requested second round is
combined using instantaneous-CSI MRC,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf Z_{\rm MRC}=\frac{|h_1|^2\widetilde{\mathbf Z}_1+
 |h_2|^2\widetilde{\mathbf Z}_2}{|h_1|^2+|h_2|^2}.
 \label{eq:mrc}
\end{equation}
Let $a\in\{0,1\}$ denote a NACK after the first-round receiver action.  The
number of physical rounds is $1+a$, so the design goal is to reduce
$\mathbb E[a]$ relative to adaptive HARQ while preserving service quality.
Before confirmatory evaluation we set matched-quality tolerances of
$\Delta{\rm PSNR}\geq-0.02$ dB and
$\Delta{\rm LPIPS}\leq0.003$.  This separates the communication claim from a
pure reconstruction claim: a receiver that improves an image but does not
avoid a round has not achieved the primary objective.

\section{FlowHARQ}
\subsection{Reliability-Anchored Partial Flow}
A reliability head outputs token corruption probabilities
$q_i=g_q(\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i},\mathbf c_{\rm rx})$, where
$\mathbf c_{\rm rx}$ includes nominal SNR, speed, CSI age, instantaneous gain,
Jakes correlation, and effective SNR.  At inference,
$m_i=\mathbb I[q_i\geq\tau_r]$.  During training, the target mask is the
top-$\kappa$ token-error subset computed from the clean latent.
Specifically, with
$e_i=d^{-1}\|\mathbf z_i-\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}\|_2^2$, the oracle mask
contains the largest $\kappa N$ errors.  A balanced tokenwise BCE trains
$g_q$ to reproduce this mask; the clean latent and oracle mask are discarded
at test time.

For masked tokens, we use the straight conditional path
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf z_i(t)=\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}
 +t m_i(\mathbf z_i-\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}),\quad
 \mathbf u_i=\mathbf z_i-\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}.
\end{equation}
The velocity field is conditioned on the received tokens, receiver CSI, mask,
and a pooled anchor computed only from reliable tokens.  Its objective is
\begin{align}
 s_i&=\max\!\left(1,\sqrt{d^{-1}\|\mathbf u_i\|_2^2}\right),\\
 \mathcal L_{\rm FM}&=\mathbb E_t
 \frac{\sum_i m_i\,\rho_{0.1}\!\left((v_\psi-\mathbf u_i)/s_i\right)}
 {\sum_i m_i},
 \label{eq:fm}
\end{align}
where $\rho_{0.1}$ is elementwise smooth-$L_1$ averaged over token dimensions.
This scale-normalized robust objective prevents rare zero-forcing deep-fade
residuals from dominating training.  We additionally optimize image $L_1$
after repair; neither target nor clean latent is available at inference.
Midpoint Euler integration uses $K$ evaluations.  A final projection enforces
\begin{equation}
 \bar{\mathbf z}_i=(1-m_i)\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}
 +m_i\mathbf z_i^{\rm FM},
 \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
so FM cannot alter a token declared reliable.

\subsection{Post-Flow HARQ Decision}
A quality head predicts log-MSE from the repaired tokens, unreliability scores,
and receiver CSI, then converts it to predicted PSNR $\widehat q$.  FlowHARQ
ACKs when $\widehat q\geq q_0$ and otherwise decodes \eqref{eq:mrc}.  The
DeepJSCC transmitter is unchanged.  The total training loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\lambda_f\mathcal L_{\rm FM}+\lambda_r\mathcal L_{\rm rel}
 +\lambda_q\mathcal L_{\rm quality}+\lambda_x\mathcal L_1.
\end{equation}
After training the repair network, we freeze every module except the quality
head and fine-tune it for the service boundary using
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm dec}=
 {\rm BCEWithLogits}\!\left((q_0-\widehat q)/T_q,\,
 \mathbb I[q<q_0]\right),
 \label{eq:decision}
\end{equation}
averaged over direct and repaired states.  This stage targets the operational
ACK/NACK decision rather than merely reducing global PSNR regression error.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
We initialize the encoder/decoder from a fixed SwinJSCC checkpoint and train
only RAFM, reliability, and quality heads on random $256\times256$ DIV2K
crops \cite{div2k}.  The encoder emits $256\times32$ real latent values,
equivalent to 4096 complex channel symbols and a channel-bandwidth ratio of
$1/48$.  The receiver-only reliability, flow, and quality modules add 0.479 M
trainable parameters to the frozen 21.83 M-parameter codec.
The reliability, flow, and initial quality modules are optimized for 40 epochs
with AdamW, mixed precision, batch size 2, learning rate $2\!\times\!10^{-4}$,
weight decay $10^{-4}$, and gradient norm 5.  We mask $\kappa=0.35$ of tokens,
set $\lambda_f=1$, $\lambda_r=0.5$, $\lambda_q=0.2$, and
$\lambda_x=1$.  The decision stage runs for 10 further epochs at
$10^{-4}$ using equal weights on log-MSE regression and
\eqref{eq:decision}; only the quality head is trainable.
Training samples SNR in $[0,15]$ dB, speed in $[0,120]$ km/h, and CSI age in
$[0,5]$ ms.  Evaluation uses SNR $\{0,3,6,9,12,15\}$ dB, speeds
$\{30,60,90,120\}$ km/h, a 1 ms HARQ interval, and Rayleigh fading.  The first
20 validation images form a calibration split; the remaining 80 are never used
for model, mask, ODE-step, threshold, or bias selection.

The service target is fixed at $q_0=24$ dB.  On the calibration split, a
conservative grid $K\in\{1,2,4\}$ and $\tau_r\in\{.8,.9\}$ is searched, and
one scalar bias is fitted for each quality state.  The frozen configuration is
$K=\SelectedFlowSteps{}$ and $\tau_r=\SelectedMaskThreshold{}$.  The
target-aware stage then fine-tunes only the quality head; the repair, mask,
codec, grid, and service target remain fixed.  We compare direct DeepJSCC, full two-round
HARQ, FM without HARQ, adaptive HARQ, FlowHARQ, and oracle variants whose NACK
uses ground-truth PSNR.  Every method shares the same first- and second-round
channel draws.  We report PSNR, SSIM \cite{ssim}, LPIPS \cite{lpips}, physical
retransmission rate, mean rounds, and GPU latency.  Confidence intervals are
two-sided 95\% Student-$t$ intervals over independent training-seed means,
after averaging channel seeds; paired effects are FlowHARQ minus adaptive HARQ
within each training seed.  The main study uses \NumTrainSeeds{} training
seeds, \NumChannelSeeds{} fresh channel seeds, and \NumTestImages{} held-out
DIV2K images.  Kodak24 is evaluated with three further channel seeds after all
choices are frozen, without refitting.

\section{Discussion}
\subsection{Communication Interpretation}
Adaptive HARQ requests a second round for 0.742 of DIV2K cases, whereas
FlowHARQ requests it for 0.729; mean physical rounds therefore change from
1.742 to 1.729.  This is a 1.75\% reduction in second-round requests, not a
1.30\% reduction in total symbols.  Relative to full HARQ, both adaptive
schemes avoid many more rounds; the contribution here is the incremental
saving obtained by inserting receiver computation before an already adaptive
decision.  On Kodak24 the corresponding second-round reduction is 2.80\%
(0.724 to 0.703), suggesting that the effect is not tied to DIV2K image
content.

The oracle decisions provide a useful sanity check.  On DIV2K, replacing the
direct state with the repaired state changes oracle NACK from 0.756 to 0.745,
an absolute 1.09-point reduction.  The learned-head reduction of 1.30 points
has the same scale and direction.  Thus the supported effect is not created
solely by a favorable scalar decision bias, although per-seed calibration
metrics remain mixed.

\subsection{SNR and Mobility}
The mean retransmission delta is favorable at every tested SNR.  Its paired
interval excludes zero at 0, 9, 12, and 15 dB, but not at 3 and 6 dB, where
training-seed variability is larger.  The result should therefore be read as
an aggregate operating-range claim rather than uniform significance in every
channel bin.  Across 30--120 km/h, mean deltas remain narrowly between
$-1.25$ and $-1.34$ points.  We find no evidence that the saving grows with
speed in this flat-fading experiment; demonstrating a mobility advantage
requires richer delay/Doppler channels rather than extrapolation from the
present simulator.

\subsection{Why Partial Transport Matters}
The identity projection in \eqref{eq:identity} is more than a computational
shortcut.  It prevents the generative module from revising evidence that the
reliability head considers trustworthy, limiting the surface on which
hallucination can occur.  The conservative selected threshold masks only high
unreliability tokens, while the robust objective handles the large residuals
created by rare fades.  The one-step control shows that identical architecture,
mask supervision, and residual targets are insufficient: conditioning on
intermediate time and integrating the learned trajectory is necessary for the
observed calibration-split repair gain.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
FlowHARQ currently uses a flat-fading simulation, a fixed DeepJSCC backbone,
and full rather than token-selective physical retransmission.  The quality head
requires a small held-out calibration split, and its balanced accuracy and ECE
do not improve for every training seed.  The measured saving is modest
(roughly 1--2 percentage points), while receiver compute is not free.
Nevertheless, the paired DIV2K interval excludes zero, the independent
Kodak24 interval does likewise, and both satisfy the predeclared
matched-quality tolerances.  Within this controlled scope, robust partial
latent transport therefore substitutes for a statistically supported fraction
of physical HARQ rounds.  Token-selective retransmission, jointly trained
codecs, and measured vehicular channels remain future work.
