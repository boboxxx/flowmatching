\begin{abstract}
Hybrid automatic repeat request (HARQ) improves neural joint source--channel
coding reliability at the cost of another channel round.  This work asks
whether receiver computation can avoid some of those transmissions.  We
propose FlowHARQ, a receiver-side virtual retransmission stage that detects
unreliable DeepJSCC latent tokens and transports only those tokens toward the
clean latent distribution using reliability-anchored flow matching (RAFM).
Reliable tokens are invariant by construction.  A calibrated post-flow quality
head requests a physical second round only when repair remains insufficient;
when requested, both rounds are combined using instantaneous-CSI maximum-ratio
combining.  On a held-out DIV2K protocol with \NumTrainSeeds{} training seeds,
\NumChannelSeeds{} channel seeds, and \NumTestImages{} test images, FlowHARQ
changes the physical retransmission rate by \MainRetxDelta{} percentage points
(95\% CI [\MainRetxCILow{}, \MainRetxCIHigh{}]) relative to adaptive HARQ,
while changing PSNR by \MainPSNRDelta{} dB (95\% CI
[\MainPSNRCILow{}, \MainPSNRCIHigh{}]).  These results test the central premise
that flow matching can act as a zero-airtime HARQ round rather than merely a
generative image decoder.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Deep joint source--channel coding (DeepJSCC) maps images directly to channel
symbols and degrades gracefully with SNR \cite{deepjscc}.  Transformer
backbones further improve high-resolution transmission and channel
adaptability \cite{swinjscc}.  Reliability remains difficult in severe fading:
always retransmitting wastes airtime, whereas an erroneous ACK produces a poor
reconstruction.  Recent semantic HARQ methods learn when or what to retransmit
\cite{djscc_h,fineharq}, but recovery still consumes another wireless round.

Flow matching (FM) learns a continuous vector field without simulating an ODE
during training \cite{flowmatching}.  Existing FM communication receivers use
FM for global reconstruction or refinement \cite{flowsem,rcbfm}.  Their goal is
better or faster decoding, including channel-aware latent transport
\cite{channel_lfm}, not deciding whether a physical retransmission can be
skipped.  Conversely, generative semantic HARQ has been explored for text
through stochastic VAE latents \cite{genharq}, leaving image DeepJSCC and FM
unconnected at the HARQ decision point.

We introduce \emph{virtual retransmission}: the receiver first repairs only
unreliable semantic latents with FM and then issues ACK/NACK.  The contribution
is one system-level idea with three parts: (i) receiver-side FM before HARQ,
(ii) reliability-anchored partial transport that exactly preserves trusted
latents, and (iii) a calibrated post-flow quality decision evaluated against
adaptive and oracle HARQ under paired, time-correlated fading.  The intended
operating point is matched reconstruction quality with fewer physical rounds.

\section{System Model}
For image $\mathbf x$, a SwinJSCC encoder emits $N$ real-valued latent tokens
$\mathbf Z=E_\theta(\mathbf x)\in\mathbb R^{N\times d}$.  Adjacent latent
dimensions form complex symbols.  In HARQ round $r\in\{1,2\}$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf y_r=h_r\mathbf s+\mathbf n_r,\qquad
 \widetilde{\mathbf Z}_r=\mathcal Q^{-1}(h_r^*\mathbf y_r/|h_r|^2),
 \label{eq:channel}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal Q^{-1}$ restores token scale and the receiver knows $h_r$.
Fading between rounds follows
\begin{equation}
 h_2=\rho_\Delta h_1+\sqrt{1-\rho_\Delta^2}\,e,quad
 \rho_\Delta=J_0(2\pi f_D\Delta),
 \label{eq:jakes}
\end{equation}
with $e\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,1)$ and HARQ interval $\Delta$.  Thus speed changes
the joint, not merely marginal, round statistics.  A requested second round is
combined using instantaneous-CSI MRC,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf Z_{\rm MRC}=\frac{|h_1|^2\widetilde{\mathbf Z}_1+
 |h_2|^2\widetilde{\mathbf Z}_2}{|h_1|^2+|h_2|^2}.
 \label{eq:mrc}
\end{equation}

\section{FlowHARQ}
\subsection{Reliability-Anchored Partial Flow}
A reliability head outputs token corruption probabilities
$q_i=g_q(\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i},\mathbf c_{\rm rx})$, where
$\mathbf c_{\rm rx}$ includes nominal SNR, speed, CSI age, instantaneous gain,
and effective SNR.  At inference,
$m_i=\mathbb I[q_i\geq\tau_r]$.  During training, the target mask is the
top-$\kappa$ token-error subset computed from the clean latent.

For masked tokens, we use the straight conditional path
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf z_i(t)=\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}
 +t m_i(\mathbf z_i-\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}),\quad
 \mathbf u_i=\mathbf z_i-\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}.
\end{equation}
The velocity field is conditioned on the received tokens, receiver CSI, mask,
and a pooled anchor computed only from reliable tokens.  Its objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\rm FM}=\mathbb E_t\frac{\sum_i m_i
 \|v_\psi(\mathbf z_i(t),t,\mathbf c_{\rm rx},\mathbf Z_{\rm rel})
 -\mathbf u_i\|_2^2}{\sum_i m_i}.
 \label{eq:fm}
\end{equation}
Midpoint Euler integration uses $K$ evaluations.  A final projection enforces
\begin{equation}
 \bar{\mathbf z}_i=(1-m_i)\widetilde{\mathbf z}_{1,i}
 +m_i\mathbf z_i^{\rm FM},
 \label{eq:identity}
\end{equation}
so FM cannot alter a token declared reliable.

\subsection{Post-Flow HARQ Decision}
A quality head predicts log-MSE from the repaired tokens, unreliability scores,
and receiver CSI, then converts it to predicted PSNR $\widehat q$.  FlowHARQ
ACKs when $\widehat q\geq q_0$ and otherwise decodes \eqref{eq:mrc}.  The
DeepJSCC transmitter is unchanged.  The total training loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L=\lambda_f\mathcal L_{\rm FM}+\lambda_r\mathcal L_{\rm rel}
 +\lambda_q\mathcal L_{\rm quality}+\lambda_x\mathcal L_1.
\end{equation}

\section{Experimental Protocol}
We initialize the encoder/decoder from a fixed SwinJSCC checkpoint and train
only RAFM, reliability, and quality heads on random $256\times256$ DIV2K
crops \cite{div2k}.  The encoder emits $256\times32$ real latent values,
equivalent to 4096 complex channel symbols and a channel-bandwidth ratio of
$1/48$.  The receiver-only reliability, flow, and quality modules add 0.479 M
trainable parameters to the frozen 21.83 M-parameter codec.
Training samples SNR in $[0,15]$ dB, speed in $[0,120]$ km/h, and CSI age in
$[0,5]$ ms.  Evaluation uses SNR $\{0,3,6,9,12,15\}$ dB, speeds
$\{30,60,90,120\}$ km/h, a 1 ms HARQ interval, and Rayleigh fading.  The first
20 validation images form a calibration split; the remaining 80 are never used
for model, mask, ODE-step, threshold, or bias selection.

Calibration chooses $K\in\{1,2,4,8\}$ and
$\tau_r\in\{0,.5,.65,.8,.9\}$ and a service target
$q_0\in\{22,24,26\}$ dB, then fits one scalar bias for each quality head.
The frozen configuration is $K=\SelectedFlowSteps{}$ and
$\tau_r=\SelectedMaskThreshold{}$.  We compare direct DeepJSCC, full two-round
HARQ, FM without HARQ, adaptive HARQ, FlowHARQ, and oracle variants whose NACK
uses ground-truth PSNR.  Every method shares the same first- and second-round
channel draws.  We report PSNR, SSIM \cite{ssim}, LPIPS \cite{lpips}, physical
retransmission rate, mean rounds, and GPU latency.  Confidence intervals are
two-sided 95\% Student-$t$ intervals over independent training-seed means,
after averaging channel seeds; paired effects are FlowHARQ minus adaptive HARQ
within each training seed.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
FlowHARQ currently uses a flat-fading simulation, a fixed DeepJSCC backbone,
and full rather than token-selective physical retransmission.  The quality head
requires a small held-out calibration split, and receiver compute is not free.
Within this controlled scope, the experiments test whether partial latent FM
can substitute for some physical HARQ rounds.  The central claim is supported
only if the retransmission confidence interval excludes zero while PSNR and
LPIPS remain within the predeclared matched-quality tolerances; otherwise the
result should be reported as a negative or inconclusive finding.
